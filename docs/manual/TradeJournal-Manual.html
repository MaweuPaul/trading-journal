Reference guide

Everything TradeJournal does, page by page.

TradeJournal is a self-hosted journal that syncs your closed trades from MT5 or cTrader, then helps you turn that history into an edge — through statistics, a chart-annotated pre-trade checklist, and a coach that looks for the psychological patterns behind your P&L.

00

Getting started

The shortest path from a fresh account to seeing your first synced trade on the Dashboard.

The two ways trades get in

  • MT5 — via the free Desktop Sync App, a small Windows program that logs into your MT5 terminal in the background and pushes closed trades to your journal. It never places trades; it only reads history.
  • cTrader — via an official OAuth connection. You approve access on cTrader's own site, and the journal pulls trade history, balances, and candlestick data directly from cTrader's API — no desktop app needed.

First-time setup

  1. Create your accountSign up with your name, email, and a password (8+ characters). You're taken straight to the Dashboard.
  2. Connect a broker accountOpen Accounts in the sidebar and either add an MT5 login (you'll need its investor/read-only password) or connect cTrader through the OAuth button.
  3. Start syncingFor MT5: generate your personal sync key, install the Desktop Sync App, paste the key in, and click Start Sync. For cTrader: click Sync Now whenever you want fresh trades.
  4. Watch the Dashboard fill inBalance, win rate, and the equity curve populate automatically once trades arrive. Everything else — journaling, playbooks, analytics — builds on top of that history.
Good to know

Trades themselves are read-only everywhere in the app — TradeJournal never edits your price, lot size, or P&L. What you add on top (notes, grades, playbook answers, screenshots, tags) is the "journal" part.

Controls

  • Time range — All Time, Today, 7/30 Days, 3/6 Months.
  • View: Tickets vs. Setups — Setups rolls re-entry trades up under their original parent, so a scaled-in position counts as one setup instead of three separate tickets.
  • Account selector — appears once you've connected more than one broker account; scopes every number on the page to just that account.

Cash flow & equity curve

  • A running total of Deposits, Withdrawals, and Net Cash Flow for the selected range, with Deposit / Withdrawal buttons for logging manual cash movements (amount, date, note). Manually-logged entries can be deleted; entries synced from your broker cannot.
  • The Equity Curve plots balance over time — hover any point for that day's exact balance and P&L.

Maintenance tools

  • Resync — requests a fresh pull from your broker (the Desktop Sync App or cTrader connection needs to be active).
  • Remove duplicates — de-dupes any doubled-up trade rows, with a confirmation first.
  • Fix Balances — re-anchors every trade's running balance from a current MT5 balance you type in. Pick a single account first if you have more than one connected.
Tip

A warning banner appears whenever the numbers on screen are blending more than one broker account — balance and drawdown aren't meaningful mixed together, so switch to the account selector when you see it.

02/trades

Trades

Your full trade log — and the place where journaling, playbook answers, and screenshots actually get attached to each trade.

Finding trades

  • Filter by Symbol, Direction, Account type (Live/Demo), Trading account, and a date range — filters are saved in the URL, so a filtered view can be bookmarked or shared.
  • Group by session clusters trades under Tokyo / London / London–NY Overlap / New York / Pacific headers, each with its own trade count, net P&L, and win rate.
  • Re-entries appear nested under their parent trade (indented, tagged "RE-ENT of #ticket"); trades sharing a position get a colored stripe down the left edge.

The Journal Drawer

Click any row to open it. This is where a closed trade turns into a journaled one.

  • Post-Trade Review — Analysis Grade and Execution Grade (A–F), scored separately so you can see when good analysis met poor execution, or vice versa.
  • Strategy Playbook — attach a published playbook; your answers to its rule checklist are saved per-trade, organized into phase tabs with an "X / Y answered" counter. Rules that require a screenshot accept a pasted image (Ctrl+V) or file upload.
  • Inherit From Pre-Trade Analysis — if you ran a Pre-Trade Analysis for this setup beforehand, one click pulls those answers and notes straight into the trade's journal.
  • Setup details — setup type, confirmation tags, planned Risk:Reward, confidence score (1–10), exit reason, and whether you manually intervened on SL/TP.
  • Re-entry linking — mark a trade as a re-entry, search for its parent by ticket number, and note why you re-entered. Re-entries skip the full playbook/notes section, since their result rolls into the parent's.
  • Trade Replay — opens a candlestick chart of exactly what happened, with Prev/Next buttons to step to the next trade without closing the modal.
Not here

There's no manual trade entry, price/lot editing, deletion, bulk actions, or CSV export on this page — trades are read-only records from your broker. (Full exports live under Backup.)

03/calendar

Calendar

A month grid of daily P&L, for spotting the days and weeks that actually make or break your month.

  • Monthly summary: Net P&L, Consistency Score (% of green days), Best Day, Worst Day.
  • Each day cell shows trade count, net $ (green/red), and either avg R or win rate for that day.
  • Filter by Symbol, Setup Grade (F is labeled "Boredom" setups), and Trading account.
  • A weekly rollup strip below the grid totals each row's P&L and active trading days.
  • Click any day with trades to open a drawer listing that day's trades, each with its own Replay button.
Tip

The small "Jump to:" date field does double duty — pick a date and it both flips the calendar to that month and opens that day's trade drawer in one step. Empty days aren't clickable, so nothing happens if you click a blank cell.

06/coach

Edge Coach

Diagnoses the psychology and discipline patterns behind your results — what's actually stopping you from executing your own plan.

What it surfaces

  • Early Exits — how many trades you closed before target, and the R/$ that likely cost you.
  • Plan Adherence — % of your own execution checklist you actually followed, shown as a progress ring.
  • Analysis vs. Execution Grade Gap — flags when your analysis quality outpaces your execution ("a psychology gap, not a strategy problem").
  • Manual Intervention — compares outcomes on trades where you touched SL/TP mid-trade vs. left them alone.
  • Demo vs. Live Comparison, Strategy Versions (performance per published playbook version, so edits don't blend with old results), and Lifecycle Stage Compliance.
  • What Works — Rule Evidence — win rate per specific playbook answer, tagged strong / developing / insufficient by sample size.
  • Confidence vs. Actual Win Rate — tests whether your gut-feel confidence rating actually predicts outcomes.
Note

Almost every panel here only appears once you've logged the field it depends on (exit reason, grades, confidence rating, live/demo tag…). This page rewards thorough journaling more than any other.

07/rules

Playbooks

Build the rulebook your trades get measured against — a versioned checklist spanning a trade's full lifecycle.

The nine lifecycle stages

1 Concept2 Market context3 Setup & location 4 Entry confirmation5 Risk plan6 Execution 7 Trade management8 Exit9 Post-trade review

Building a strategy

  • New strategy starts a blank draft — nothing saves until you publish.
  • Add a stage via Add recommended stage (pre-filled with 3 starter rules for that category) or Add empty stage. Stages can be renamed, reordered, recategorized, or removed.
  • Add rule inside any stage: set its label, answer type (Yes/No, Number, Text, Single/Multiple choice — with sub-formats like Percentage, Currency, R:R, Duration, Rating, Date/Time), and whether it's required.
  • Each rule's More options panel holds: tool role (Observation, Confirmation, Entry trigger, Decision gate, Invalidation, Risk limit, Management action, Exit signal, Review prompt), what happens when it's not satisfied (record only / warn / skip trade / stop session), applicable timeframes, help text, and a trade-screenshot requirement with min/max image counts.
  • Stages and rules can each carry optional reference example images to teach what a "valid" vs. "invalid" setup looks like.

Publishing

  • Every block/rule needs a name, and you must write a change note of at least 20 characters describing what changed and why — this becomes a permanent part of that version's record.
  • Publishing creates a new immutable version; older versions are never overwritten, so past trades keep pointing at the exact checklist they were judged against.
  • Archive retires a strategy without deleting its history. Read playbook opens a clean, read-only view of the published version.
Tip

The strategy panel lists any open Pre-Trade Analyses already started against it, so you can jump back into one mid-setup instead of starting over.

08/pretrade

Pre-Trade Analysis

Run your playbook's checklist before you enter — on a live chart you can draw on — then carry the answers into the real trade once it's taken.

Starting one

  • From Playbooks, click Start Pre-Trade Analysis on any strategy. It locks in that strategy's current published version and shows only its Plan-phase stages — Concept, Market context, Setup, Entry confirmation, and Risk plan — since Execution/Management/Exit/Review can't apply before a trade exists.
  • Answer the same rule checklist you'd fill in on a real trade. Screenshot-required rules simply note that confirmation will be requested later, once inherited into an actual trade.

The chart

A candlestick chart loads for whatever symbol you type — with the same drawing toolset as Trade Replay, so you can mark up key levels yourself instead of the app guessing them for you.

Zone (rectangle)Trend linePrice level ArrowText noteImage evidence Select / moveUndoDelete
  • Type a symbol and a datalist suggests real names from your connected broker account, so you're not guessing a spelling like "USTEC" vs. "NAS100" vs. "US100" yourself.
  • Choose a timeframe (1m–1D) and lookback window (2–30 days) to bring the right amount of history into view.
  • Use the drawing tools to mark Previous Day High/Low, Previous Week High/Low, and the Asian / London session highs and lows — then look for price to either sweep through one (a liquidity grab that reverses) or continue through it (a breakout).
  • Everything — chart symbol, timeframe, and every annotation — autosaves a moment after you stop drawing.

Bringing it into a real trade

  • When the trade actually happens and syncs in, open its Journal Drawer and use Inherit From Pre-Trade Analysis to pull the playbook answers and notes across in one click.
By design

TradeJournal never calculates PDH/PDL or session levels for you — marking them is a manual, deliberate act, the same way you'd draw on a chart in your trading platform. The chart just gives you a canvas.

09/knowledge

Knowledge Base

A free-form, searchable notebook for anything that isn't tied to one specific trade — broker quirks, market-structure ideas, session notes.

  • New Entry opens a drawer with a Title, free-text Notes body, and comma-separated Tags.
  • A live search box filters entries by title/content as you type.
  • Entries display as cards: title, a short preview, tags, screenshot count, and date.
  • Attach screenshots by uploading a file — or by pasting an image straight from your clipboard (Ctrl+V) while the entry drawer is open.
Order matters

A brand-new entry has to be saved once before you can attach screenshots to it — the drawer will prompt you to save first.

10/accounts

Accounts & syncing

Where every broker connection, the Desktop Sync App, and investor access are all managed.

Connecting MT5

  1. Add accountGive it a label, your MT5 login number, broker server, and your MT5 investor (read-only) password — never your trading password. Optionally map journal symbol names to broker-specific ones (e.g. XAUUSD:XAUUSDm) under Advanced symbol mapping.
  2. Generate your sync keyUnder "Your personal sync key," click Generate / rotate. It's shown once — copy it immediately.
  3. Install the Desktop Sync AppDownload it, paste in your sync key, and click Start Sync. This small Windows app is what actually logs into MT5 and pushes trades in the background — the account record alone doesn't sync anything by itself.

Connecting cTrader

  • Connect cTrader redirects to cTrader's own OAuth approval page. On return, pick which of your cTrader accounts under that login to actually connect.
  • Each connected account gets Sync Now (pulls new trades and deposits/withdrawals) and Disconnect (stops syncing; already-synced trades stay in your history).
  • cTrader is also what powers Trade Replay's candlestick charts and the Pre-Trade Analysis chart — it matters even if your trade history itself comes from MT5.

Account cards

Each card shows connection status (Connected / Needs attention / Waiting for agent / Disabled), trade count, balance, last sync time, and a sync on/off toggle. MT5 cards can be edited or archived directly; cTrader cards are managed through the cTrader section instead.

Sharing investor access

Per account, not global — expand the Investor panel on any account card.

  • Enable investor access generates a numeric account code and a one-time password (shown once, like the MT5 investor password).
  • Copy investor link gives you a shareable /investor/login URL; copy the password separately and send it through a different channel.
  • Rotate password issues a new one (the old one stops working immediately); Revoke access disables the link entirely.
11/archive

Backup & export

Health-check your stored data and pull a full export — this page reads and downloads, it doesn't restore.

  • Database Status — live badge: Database Connected, or Offline (Local Cache) if the app has fallen back to a local copy. Shows total trades, account events, and your oldest/latest trade dates.
  • Local JSON Mirror — the server automatically mirrors all trade data to its own disk on startup and every 24 hours, as a safety net if the live database goes down.
  • Screenshot redundancy — every screenshot is backed by three layers: metadata embedded in the image itself, a sidecar metadata file, and offline disk storage that auto-uploads once you're back online.
  • Download JSON — a full backup (trades, account events, screenshot URLs) suitable for restoring into a fresh database.
  • Download CSV — trade fields formatted for Excel or Sheets.
One-way only

There's no restore/import button on this page, even though the JSON export is restore-capable. Treat this page as export-only.

Boundaries

An investor session can't reach any other page — no accounts, no playbooks, no journal notes, no other accounts you own — and can't edit or upload anything. It's a separate login token from your own, so it can't be reused to access the owner side of the app.

Glossary & quick reference

Terms that show up across the app without much explanation in the moment.

TermWhat it means here
R / Avg RResult measured as a multiple of what you risked. A trade risking $100 that made $250 is "2.5R." Only calculated for trades with a recorded stop.
Setup vs. TicketA "ticket" is one broker execution; a "setup" groups a ticket with any of its re-entries so scaled-in positions count once.
PDH / PDLPrevious Day High / Previous Day Low — a key level to watch for a sweep or continuation.
PWH / PWLPrevious Week High / Previous Week Low — the same idea, one timeframe up.
SweepPrice pushes briefly past a level to grab liquidity, then reverses — as opposed to a genuine breakout.
ContinuationPrice breaks a level and keeps going in that direction.
Plan Adherence% of your own execution checklist you actually followed on a trade.
Analysis vs. Execution GradeTwo separate A–F scores — how good your read was, versus how well you carried it out.
Sample statusEdge Coach tags evidence as strong / developing / insufficient based on how many trades back it up — a built-in caution against small-sample conclusions.
Investor passwordA broker or TradeJournal password that only ever grants read access — shown once, never retrievable again after that.
Playbook versionAn immutable snapshot of a strategy's rules, created every time you publish — past trades keep pointing at the exact checklist they were judged against.
